How classification works, and its limits

SafeScale matches explicit industry, technology, geography, and technique terms in publisher headlines and RSS descriptions. Each tag retains its matching words and whether it used a headline alone or RSS context. Full articles are not analysed. Items may have several tags; uncertain dimensions stay unclassified.

Sector packs use a separate technology map to suggest relevance. For example, a Windows item may be useful to a retailer operating Windows endpoints even if no retailer is named. An India-relevant item can describe a shared technology risk without evidence of Indian victims. Startup relevance describes operational fit, not the size of any reported victim.

“Explicit term match” describes confidence in detecting those words, not confidence in victim attribution or the truth of a report. Read the original source, check versions and exposure, and corroborate findings. Headline/URL deduplication cannot reliably merge every report about the same event.

CISA KEV entries confirm known exploitation according to CISA. They are catalog entries, not new breach reports. Their dates are catalog-addition dates; U.S. federal remediation deadlines are not Indian compliance deadlines. An unmatched item is still visible under unclassified filters.

For Indian businesses & startups

Local guidance. Practical preparation.

Check CERT-In for current Indian advisories and incident-reporting directions. Establish an incident owner, preserve useful identity and system logs, and review vendor guidance for technologies you actually use. Source relevance is not a finding that your business is affected.
